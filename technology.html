Technology

How DragonSpire is built.

DragonSpire has a front of house and a backend. Dee Emma is the front of house: she hears and speaks to players in Discord and on the web. Cory is the backend, a Python core with its own database that runs the game, alongside Campaign Manager and DragonLibrary, the official databases for the world and the rules. FGU Link bridges to Fantasy Grounds on your own computer. AI models tell the story and voice it.

Memory and rules

Cory keeps the live state of each campaign in its own database. Campaign Manager holds the world: places, people and quests, written by the GM in a web app and handed to the AI game master every turn. DragonLibrary holds D&D 2024 rules content and Fantasy Grounds effect codes, looked up rather than guessed. Both run on PocketBase, and Cory uses SQLite.

Fantasy Grounds is optional

Fantasy Grounds Unity handles the mechanical crunch, inventories and character sheets, and works much like a database of the game. DragonSpire does not own or control it; FGU Link is the bridge, a small program that connects to Fantasy Grounds’ API on the copy running on your own computer. You can also play a lighter, less crunchy version in the web browser alone.

One container per campaign

Cory runs in its own Docker container for each campaign, which keeps campaigns separate from each other.
